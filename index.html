About

I'm a Senior ML Engineer at Salesforce AI Research, where I build and evaluate LLM agents: multi-agent orchestration, post-training data, tool use, and evaluations on real enterprise workflows. I led Enterprise Deep Research, an open-source deep research agent. Before that I was a founding ML engineer at Bland AI, building real-time voice agents.

Shipping these systems has made me more curious about why models behave the way they do than about pushing benchmark numbers. I've seen agents that look strong in evaluation fail on live customer workflows, and seen the choice of grader change what counts as a failure. I'm now expanding into AI safety research through independent projects on reward hacking, monitoring, and multi-agent oversight.

Under all of this is a broader curiosity about intelligence itself. I've practiced meditation seriously for years, and contemplative practice has left me with questions about attention, perception, and how intentions become actions. Those questions keep showing up in how I think about models. I try to treat the parallels between minds and models as hypotheses to test, not equivalences.

Research Interests

Reward hacking & specification gaming
Why do models learn to optimize proxies instead of the objectives we meant?
Learned objectives
What do models actually learn during training, and how could it differ from what we intended?
Mechanistic interpretability
Can we explain a model's decisions from its internals, not only from its outputs?
Generalization under pressure
Why does behavior change under distribution shift or stronger optimization?
Scalable oversight
How do we evaluate systems that may outperform us in the domain being evaluated?

I like to approach these empirically: train models, run controlled experiments, and look closely at both behavior and internals. I value foundational work even when its use isn't clear yet. I'm especially drawn to problems where safety and capability line up. Understanding reward hacking, for example, should give us agents that are both safer and better at the task we actually wanted done.

Reading lately: Sleeper Agents and alignment stress-testing · emergent misalignment · representation engineering · chain-of-thought monitorability · credit assignment in human learning and RL

Independent Safety Research 2026

  • Reward hacking in coding agents. 200 runs of four models on 25 SWE tasks with contradictory specs, comparing cheating and disclosure under pressure. Two methods for labeling whether a model knew it was cheating disagreed on 10 of 30 passing runs.
  • Swarm Early Warning (with Isahan Khan). A 100-episode monitoring study that compares activation-based signals with monitors that see only the context visible before an agent acts.
  • Agent Trust Guard. Provenance-aware monitoring for forged authorization across 280 live agent conversations, measuring attack attribution, false quarantines, and legitimate task completion.
  • The Price of Permission. Consent-rule and payoff ablations for multi-agent task allocation. Stricter consent cut unauthorized changes but cost task completion.
  • Swarm on Fire. Attributing covert agendas across 83 simulated agent swarms (71,600 posts), separating coordinated fronts from independent agents that share the same agenda.
  • Simple Freedom. Self-play behavior of 10 models over 132 conversations, scored by five blinded judges, with an audit of judge and harness confounds.
